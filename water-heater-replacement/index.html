While you wait, close the cold water valve to the heater and put towels or a wet vacuum on the water.

Replacement means removing the old heater, setting a new one of the right type and size, reconnecting water, gas or power and venting, and testing it under working pressure. It is a bigger job than a repair, and the size you choose affects your bills and your hot water for the next decade.

Is it a repair or a replacement

Some signs make the decision easy:

  • Water leaks from the tank body or the seam at the bottom.
  • The unit is past the age listed on its rating label and has had more than one repair.
  • Hot water runs out sooner than it used to and the tank rumbles when it heats.
  • Rust-colored hot water persists after the faucet runs for a minute.

Other cases are less clear. A heater with a failed thermostat and no other trouble can be repaired. Our water heater repair page shows which faults are worth fixing.

Sizing the new heater

Size is the choice people get wrong most often. A tank that is too small runs out, and one that is too large wastes fuel keeping extra water hot. The plumber looks at three things.

  1. How many people live in the home and how many bathrooms run at once.
  2. The fuel you have now, gas or electric, and whether a switch is practical.
  3. The space and venting available, since a larger tank needs more room.

A first-hour rating on the heater's yellow label is a better guide than the gallon number alone. Ask what it is for the models you are offered, and compare it with your busiest morning.

What the installation includes

A proper replacement is more than setting a tank in the old spot:

  • Draining and disconnecting the old heater and carrying it out.
  • New flexible connectors, a new relief valve and a discharge pipe to code.
  • Gas line and venting checks for a gas heater, including a leak test.
  • An expansion tank where the system needs one.
  • Starting the heater, checking temperature and pressure and showing you the shutoff.

The New Jersey plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code, and that code sets the rules for relief valve discharge and similar details. Ask your plumber how permit and inspection requirements apply to your address.

Lodi basements and hot water tanks

Lodi's basements take on water in big storms. Remnants of Hurricane Ida in September 2021 left sections of the borough under several feet of water, according to CBS New York. A heater that sat in floodwater can have damaged controls even if it still lights.

If your heater was submerged, do not relight it. Have a plumber check the gas valve or electrical parts first, because water damage may not show until later. For regular flood risk, ask about raising the heater on a platform or moving it upstairs.

What to do while the old tank is failing

  • Close the cold water supply valve on top of the heater to stop the flow.
  • Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or set the gas control to pilot or off for a gas heater.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911.
  • Open a hot tap in the house to relieve pressure, and protect the floor with towels.
  • Call a plumber to plan the replacement before the tank fails completely.

Water heater replacement questions

Do you replace water heaters at night or on weekends in Lodi?

Yes. A failed or leaking tank is treated as an emergency, and the line is open 24/7. Same-day replacement depends on parts and the schedule, and the plumber tells you plainly what is possible.

How long does a replacement take?

Most standard swaps take a few hours once the new heater is on site. Changes to venting, gas lines or electric service can add time.

Should I switch from a tank to a tankless unit?

It can make sense, but it changes the gas line, venting and sometimes the electrical service. See tankless water heater for the trade-offs.

Will you take away the old water heater?

Yes. Removal of the old unit is part of the visit.

Can you replace a heater in a condo or apartment?

Often yes, but you may need building approval for gas, venting or access. Call with the details.
