Stopping the water in the first five minutes

Do these in order:

  1. Turn off the main water shutoff, usually near the meter in the basement or a utility room. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops.
  2. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the pipes.
  3. Switch off the water heater at its breaker or gas control so it does not run dry.
  4. Turn off electricity to the wet area at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  5. Photograph the damage for your insurance claim before cleanup begins.
  6. Move belongings away, lift furniture off wet floors and start removing water with towels or a wet vacuum.

If you cannot find the main valve or it will not turn, say so on the call. The plumber can talk you through other options. For a leak on the street side of the meter, the Passaic Valley Water Commission, which supplies Lodi, runs a 24/7 emergency line at 973-340-4300.

Frozen pipes: thaw safely, then check

A frozen pipe has not burst yet, but it may. If you turn on a faucet and nothing comes out, a section is probably frozen. Keep the faucet open so melting water can drain, and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or heating pad.

Never use an open flame. If the pipe is split, you will see water as it thaws, and that is the moment to close the main and call.

Pipes in unheated spaces, exterior walls and basements near uninsulated windows are the most exposed. Insulation sleeves, closing foundation vents and letting a faucet drip on the coldest nights reduce the risk. If you will be away in winter, keep the heat on and have someone check the house.

How the repair goes

The plumber works in a clear order:

  • Confirm the water is off and make the area safe.
  • Open the wall or ceiling at the burst only as far as needed, using a moisture reading to find where the water is.
  • Cut out the failed section and replace it with new pipe and fittings of matching material.
  • Pressure test the repaired line and the neighboring pipe.
  • Restore water slowly and check every joint.
  • Explain what caused the failure and whether other pipes in the house are at risk.

Where the pipe is hidden, the opening is repaired separately, either by us or by your contractor. If a burst reveals corroded pipe throughout, the plumber will say so. Repeated failures are the main reason to consider repiping.

After the water stops: protecting the house

Drying matters as much as the repair. Mold can start within a day or two in wet drywall and insulation. After the plumber stops the leak, call your insurance company, run fans and dehumidifiers and ask a restoration company to check for moisture inside walls. The plumber can point out where water traveled.

Burst pipes and flooding in Lodi

Lodi sits along the Saddle River, and parts of the borough flood in heavy rain, so many residents already know what water in the basement feels like. A burst pipe is a different problem from a flood. A flood comes from outside, and a burst comes from your own plumbing.

If you are not sure which you have, shut the main anyway. If water continues to rise after the main is off, the source is outside, and our sump pump page is where to start.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the main water valve, then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines.
  • Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel safely, and stay out of rooms where water meets outlets.
  • Move belongings out of the water's path and use towels, buckets or a wet vacuum.
  • Take photos of the damage before cleanup.
  • Do not use a flame to thaw a frozen pipe.

Burst pipe questions

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Lodi?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. A burst pipe is a same-day emergency, and the plumber gives a clear price before starting.

Should I shut off the water heater?

Yes, if the main is off and the heater could run dry or the burst is on its supply. Turn off gas or power to it.

Will insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe, but not slow leaks or neglect. Call your insurer, photograph everything and keep the invoice.

How do I prevent a pipe from freezing?

Insulate exposed pipes, seal gaps in exterior walls, keep the heat on and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. Disconnect hoses from outdoor spigots in the fall.

What if I cannot find the main shutoff?

Tell the plumber when you call. We can describe where it usually sits and help you find it, and for a street-side leak the utility has an emergency line.
