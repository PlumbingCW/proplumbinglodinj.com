The plumbing problems Hackensack calls about

Hackensack is the largest municipality in Bergen County, with 46,030 residents in 2020 and 20,290 housing units across 4.35 square miles. A city this size has a wide range of plumbing needs, and a few problems are common:

  • Sewer and drain backups in apartment buildings and houses, where one blocked line affects several fixtures.
  • Basement water in low parts of the city, since Hackensack sits at about 20 feet of elevation beside a tidal river.
  • Water heater failures, from tank leaks to no hot water in winter.
  • Leaks inside walls and ceilings that show up as stains or high bills.
  • Commercial drain and restroom problems for shops and offices near Main Street.

For clogs and backups, see drain cleaning and sewer line cleaning. For hot water, start with water heater repair.

Downtown, Main Street and the hospital district

The city's downtown is a shopping and office district centered on Main Street, home to the Johnson Public Library at 274 Main Street, which opened in 1901, and the Bergen County Court House. Hackensack University Medical Center, the city's main hospital, sits at 30 Prospect Avenue. Buildings in a district like this often share walls, basements and drain lines, so one tenant's problem can become everyone's.

A restaurant or office in these blocks needs a plumber who plans around business hours and treats a backed-up floor drain as an emergency. Our commercial plumbing page explains how we handle those calls, and we can schedule maintenance outside opening hours.

Suburban streets on the west side

About a third of the city is suburban single-family neighborhoods, mostly on the west side, according to its Wikipedia profile. These houses have basements with water heaters, floor drains and sump pits. A homeowner there is more likely to call about a leaking tank, a slow main line or a sump pump that stopped during a storm.

Neighborhood names include Fairmount and North Hackensack, both unincorporated areas inside the city, and Red Hill and Cherry Hill. If you are in one of them and unsure whether we cover your block, call and we will confirm.

Water in Hackensack and what it does to pipes

Hackensack water comes from Veolia Water New Jersey's Hackensack system, public water system ID NJ0238001, which lists Hackensack City among its served towns on NJ WaterCheck. Veolia's 2025 results for the system show a highest hardness of 210 ppm, which leaves scale inside water heaters and on faucet screens. A tank heater here benefits from a yearly flush, and a tankless unit needs descaling. See tankless water heater service for the details.

Your water bill names your supplier for your address, and that is the document to trust if it disagrees with any list.

Rain, river and basement water

The Hackensack River runs through the city, and low ground close to it is where basements take on water in heavy storms. A sump pump with a battery backup is the best defense, and we cover that on the sump pump page. If sewage backs up through a floor drain while it is raining, tell the plumber, because the problem may be the municipal system and not your line.

For lasting fixes such as interior drains and backwater valves, see basement waterproofing. We are honest about what plumbing can and cannot fix.
