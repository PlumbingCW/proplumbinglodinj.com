Emergency problems we fix first

An after-hours call usually involves one of these:

  1. A sewer or floor drain that backs up into a kitchen or restroom.
  2. A burst or leaking pipe in a ceiling, a wall or a utility room.
  3. A water heater failure in a business that needs hot water to operate.
  4. A toilet or urinal that overflows and will not stop.
  5. A main water shutoff that will not close.

For these, the plumber stops the water, protects the space and gives a clear price for the repair before work begins. If the fix needs parts that are not on the truck, we say so and plan the quickest way to get you running.

Grease traps, grease lines and floor drains

Restaurants and food businesses have to manage grease. Grease interceptors and traps catch fats before they enter the sewer, and a trap that is not emptied on schedule lets grease pass into the line and clog it. A full trap smells, drains slowly and can fail an inspection.

We service grease lines with cabling and hydro jetting, and we can set up a cleaning schedule that matches your volume. Ask your landlord and the borough about any local grease or inspection requirements that apply to your building before you choose a schedule.

Backflow prevention and testing

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps water in a building from flowing back into the public supply. Many commercial properties, including those with irrigation, fire sprinklers or certain equipment, have one. These assemblies are tested by a certified tester, usually once a year, and the test result goes to the water supplier.

Which assemblies need testing and how often depends on your supplier and the local rules. Lodi is served by the Passaic Valley Water Commission, and its customer service line, 973-340-4300, can tell you what applies to your account. We can coordinate the repair if an assembly fails its test.
