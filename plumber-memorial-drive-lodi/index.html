What is on Memorial Drive

The borough's Building Department lists its address as One Memorial Drive, Room 204. A trade publication reports that Lodi Commons at 4 Memorial Drive is a shopping center owned by Urban Edge Properties.

We do not know which tenants have a plumbing problem, and we do not claim that any building here does. What we can say is that retail and food spaces share a few plumbing needs, which the rest of this page covers.

Plumbing for shops and restaurants

Restaurants and food shops depend on hot water, floor drains and kitchen drain lines that carry grease. A slow floor drain in a kitchen is easier to fix before a backup closes the business. Our commercial plumbing page explains emergency calls and maintenance, and hydro jetting cleans a full pipe wall when grease keeps coming back.

Restrooms are the other pressure point. A running toilet or a leaking faucet wastes water and annoys customers, and toilet repair and installation covers the usual fixes. Water heaters that serve a kitchen or a salon can be repaired or replaced, and water heater replacement explains the choices.

Permits and inspections near Borough Hall

Plumbing work often needs a permit, and the Building Department is on this corridor. The department's page says it conducts building, electrical and plumbing subcode inspections to enforce the Uniform Construction Code of New Jersey. It lists 973-859-7410 for the Building Department and shows weekday hours of 8:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. in its site-wide contact section. Confirm the hours before you go.

New Jersey's plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code. Ask whether your job needs a permit, and who pulls it, before work begins.

Three questions cover most permit calls. Does the job need a permit at all? Who has to apply for it, the tenant, the landlord or the plumber? And what inspection follows when the work is done? Write down the answers, because a repair that skips a required inspection can cause trouble when the space is sold or leased.

Tenants, landlords and scheduling

In a shopping center, the lease usually says who owns each pipe. Check it before you call a plumber, because a shared drain or a main line may belong to the landlord. As general advice, photograph the problem and note which units share a wall or a drain.

Plan work around opening hours. Tell us when the space is empty, and we confirm scheduling on the call.
