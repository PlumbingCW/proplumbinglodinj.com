Tankless Water Heater Cost Installed for Lodi, NJ Homeowners.

A tankless unit costs more to install than a tank in most houses. Whether it pays back depends on your gas line, your vent and your hot water habits.

Written by Lodi Pro Plumbing / Published October 15, 2026

Call (973) 488-5410.

A tankless water heater usually costs more to install than a tank, and the reason is not the unit alone. The house has to supply it with enough gas, vent its exhaust properly and deliver enough water flow to start it.

When people are surprised by a tankless quote, it is almost always one of those three. We avoid quoting a dollar figure in a guide because the real number depends on your house, but we can show where the money goes.

A tankless heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it. That means it never runs out in a long shower, it takes less floor space and it can last longer than a tank. It also means that on a cold winter day, the unit must raise the temperature of incoming water in a single pass, and that is why it needs a powerful burner and a good gas supply.

If your house already has a large gas line and a straight path to an exterior wall, the install is simpler. If the unit is going into a basement corner far from an outside wall, the vent run adds cost.

Tank versus tankless over the long run

Tankless units cost more up front and, in many homes, less to run, because they do not keep a tank of water hot all day. The savings depend on how much hot water you use. A household that uses a lot of hot water benefits more than a one-person home. A household that uses little may take many years to recover the higher installation cost.

Tankless units often have a longer expected life than tanks when maintained, and replacement parts are repairable instead of requiring a whole new unit. That can offset the higher purchase price. The ongoing cost people forget: descaling

A tankless heater has a narrow heat exchanger, and scale in the water collects on it. The unit works harder, makes more noise and eventually throws error codes. Descaling once a year in hard water keeps it healthy. The cost of a flush is small compared with the cost of a failed heat exchanger, but it recurs, so include it when you compare tank and tankless.

Install service valves on day one. They are cheap at install and expensive to add later, and they turn a descale into a quick visit. If an installer leaves them out, ask why.

Questions to ask before you buy

  1. How large is my gas line, and will it carry the full load of this unit?
  2. Where will the vent run, and what does it cost?
  3. Will the unit have service valves for flushing?
  4. What is the manufacturer's recommended maintenance, and what does it cost?
  5. How does the first-hour equivalent compare with my current tank?
  6. What does the warranty cover on the heat exchanger and on labor?

A plumber who walks your basement and answers these is more useful than one who quotes from the doorway.

Water hardness, descaling and the Lodi supply

The Passaic Valley Water Commission's 2026 report lists hardness from 106 to 242 ppm at the Little Falls plant, alkalinity from 54 to 89 ppm and pH between 7.92 and 8.38. At the high end of that hardness range, a tankless heat exchanger collects scale quickly, and a yearly flush is a sensible budget line.

Customers in the Veolia Hackensack system, which includes Maywood and Wood-Ridge, see a reported maximum of 210 ppm.

That means a Lodi buyer should treat descaling as part of ownership. Ask any installer how the unit will be flushed, and whether the quote includes the isolation valves that make it easy. The PVWC report is public if you want to check your own supply.

When tankless is a poor fit

Tankless is not right for every house:

  • A small household with low hot water use may never recoup the extra cost.
  • A house with a small gas line may need expensive upsizing.
  • A basement corner with no practical vent route adds cost or rules out the install.
  • A house with low water pressure may not trigger the burner reliably.

In those cases, a good tank is a sensible choice, and our water heater installation page covers it.

Hybrid and electric options

Heat pump water heaters are an alternative for homes without gas or that want lower electric bills. They pull heat from the surrounding air and need space and air flow. They cost more than a standard electric tank and less to run, and they cool and dehumidify the room they sit in. The plumber checks the space before recommending one.

Questions before you sign

Ask the installer which model they recommend and why, how the gas line and vent will be handled, whether the electrical work is included and what maintenance the unit needs. Ask for a written scope. A tankless unit is a larger project than a tank swap, and a clear scope prevents surprises on the day.

Questions about tankless cost

Why does tankless cost more to install than a tank?

The unit needs a bigger gas line, special venting, a condensate drain and service valves. Those extras are usually the difference.

Does a tankless heater really save money?

It can, especially in households with high hot water use. In a small household the savings may be modest. Ask the plumber to compare your actual usage.

Does hard water void a tankless warranty?

Some manufacturers require regular descaling to keep the warranty. Read the warranty and keep a record of each flush.

Can I install a tankless heater in a Lodi apartment or condo?

It depends on venting, gas supply and building rules. Call us with the layout, and we tell you what is possible.

Are there local rebates for tankless units?

Rebates change often. Check with your gas and electric utilities and the state energy programs before you buy.

Code basis. Plumbing work in New Jersey follows the state plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts the 2021 National Standard Plumbing Code. Ask which permits apply to your job and confirm with the Lodi Building Department.
